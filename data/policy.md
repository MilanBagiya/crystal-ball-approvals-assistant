# Approval & Maintenance Policy

Applies to every item submitted to the Approvals & Review queue of the site digital twin.

## Review SLAs

Safety documents, sensor specifications and anything tagged as a hazard must be reviewed within 24 hours of submission. All other items, including onboarding folders, patrol checklists, videos and layout images, must be reviewed within 72 hours. The SLA clock starts at the submission date and pauses only while an item is waiting on the submitter for requested changes.

## Content checks by type

Folders: every file opens, names follow the site naming convention, and there are no duplicate or empty files. Videos: plays end to end, is at least 720p, and has no faces or licence plates left unblurred. PDFs: text is searchable, a version number and revision date appear on the cover, and every page is legible. Images: the orientation is correct, a scale or north arrow is visible, and zone labels match the twin.

## Approve, reject or request changes

Approve when every content check passes and the item sits in the correct path. Request changes when the fixes are small and clear, such as a missing revision date, a wrong label or a low resolution, and list each fix in the comment. Reject when the item is a duplicate, belongs to another site, or contains personal data that cannot be redacted.

## Safety and sensor specifications

Every safety or sensor specification must list the equipment model, the calibration date, the next calibration due date and a named owner. A calibration older than 12 months means the item cannot be approved. Specifications must state the operating range and any alarm thresholds. Changes to an approved specification need a second reviewer.

## Escalation

Escalate to the Site Safety Lead when a safety item is close to breaching its 24 hour SLA, or when a specification shows an expired calibration. Escalate to the Digital Twin Admin when an item is stuck for more than 72 hours or cannot be opened. Escalate by email and record the escalation in the item comment.
